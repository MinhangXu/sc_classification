\coreq{What is PC8?}{core:pc8}{PC8's opposite-sign gene groups: what is it? (team report); MX: ``19 out of 20''? (A-20261010-06, -14)}

\begin{coreanswer}
\textbf{One axis that contrasts two groups of IFN-$\alpha$ genes; name it by its poles.} Oriented by its coefficient in the L1 probe ($C=10^{-4}$), malignant cells score toward TXNIP, IFITM2, TENT5A (the team's ``negative'' group) and normal cells toward IFI27, PSME2, PSMA3. It carries held-out lift in five of seven patients, but it is not identifiable as ``factor 8'': a refit of Stage~1 can spread the same direction over several factors.
\end{coreanswer}

\evidence
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item \emph{Lift}: PC8 alone gives 5.47--16.27\pp{} of held-out lift in P02, P03, P04, P07 and P09 (FA7 alone 4.81--19.87\pp), and under 1\pp{} in P01 and P06.
\item \emph{Poles} (top): the malignant pole runs from TXNIP to ELF1, the normal pole from IFI27 to USP18; interferon-stimulated genes sit on both, so the axis contrasts two parts of the response, not ``more interferon''.
\item \emph{FA7 is related, not identical}: its loadings resemble PC8's (similarity 0.69 on a 0--1 scale, below the 0.85 at which two factors are usually called fairly similar), and it has the same sign as PC8 on 19 of the 20 pole genes shown.
\item \emph{Not identifiable} (a refit can return a different single direction): PC8's variance is within 5.5\% of PC7's; without P01 in Stage~1, PC8's best single match falls to $|r|$ 0.58 while 98.8\% of it stays in the refit basis; FA7 is only loosely pinned down by the data (determinacy 0.649, below the usual 0.707 line).
\item \emph{Cell type} (bottom): malignant cells score higher within 8 of 10 predicted types and, inside one supported patient and type ($\geq$10 cells of each label), in 19 of 21 comparisons (19 of 20 in the first figure we sent Pavan, without P06's pDC2). PC8 was chosen and oriented by the malignant-vs-healthy probe: only the gap inside one patient and type is new.
\end{itemize}

\coreq{Have we integrated the protein data? Does it track PC8?}{core:protein}{Pavan: ``have you been able to integrate the protein data?''; MX: ``So the sign means how forward and reversed the rank \ldots?''; ``also include CD116$-$0.287, CD33$-$0.272, CD146 $+$0.270'' (A-20261010-03..-05, -16..-24)}

\begin{coreanswer}
\textbf{Not into the factors: Stage~1 is fit on RNA only. We use the 170 surface proteins to read them.} One Spearman $\rho$ per factor and protein, among cells of one label; its sign says whether the two rankings run the same way or in reverse (A). PC8's strongest protein correlates (those consistent across patients) mostly do not persist within cell types, so they mostly say which cell type a cell is (Table~1; B, C).
\end{coreanswer}

\evidence
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item \emph{The sign} (A): $\rho$ is the Pearson correlation of the 1,569 malignant cells' PC8 ranks and protein ranks. $\rho > 0$: the rankings run forward (CD99 $+0.222$: mean CD99 rank 585 in the lowest tenth of PC8, 932 in the highest); $\rho < 0$: in reverse (CD48 $-0.192$: 913 to 591). The sign means something only because PC8 is oriented; no healthy cell enters a malignant-cell $\rho$.
\item \emph{Two checks} (Table~1, B). \emph{Consistent across patients}: the robust rule on the middle row ($|$median$| \geq 0.20$, $\geq$5 of 7 of one sign; 5 of the 14 passing stay inside the isotype reach, 0.227). \emph{Persists within cell types}: the last row past its reach (0.120). CD48, CD116, CD33, CD146 pass only the first; CD99 only the second (a lead: 11 groups, 4 patients); CD63, CD82 both. With each cell's total ADT count held fixed (D29), only CD63 of these still clears both bands.
\item \emph{Healthy cells} (C): CD34 ($+0.245$ within patients, 7/7) and the other correlates (CD22, CD48, CD81, CD112) shrink inside cell types, none beyond $|\rho| = 0.085$; every cell passed a CD34+ sort, yet CD34 still varies with differentiation stage. \emph{Isotypes}: 9 antibodies named \texttt{Isotype-<clone>} in the data, with no human target: a noise reference, not a test.
\end{itemize}
