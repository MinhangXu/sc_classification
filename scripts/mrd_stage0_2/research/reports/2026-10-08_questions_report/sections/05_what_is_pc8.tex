\begin{answerbox}
PC8 is a single axis that contrasts two groups of IFN-$\alpha$ genes. Once PC8 is oriented by its L1 coefficient, the team's ``negative'' list (TXNIP, IFITM2, TENT5A, NCOA7, TRIM5, ELF1) is the pole that malignant cells score toward. FA7 is a related axis (Tucker $\phi$ 0.691, below the 0.85 ``fair'' similarity band). Either factor alone gives 4.81--19.87\pp{} of held-out lift in the same five supported patients (P02, P03, P04, P07, P09) and under 1\pp{} in P01 and P06.

\emph{Not identifiable} means the data pin down a small subspace (the span of PC8 and its near-tied neighbours, which every refit reproduces) but not which single direction inside it is ``factor 8'': PC8's variance is within 5.5\% of PC7's, and leaving P01 out of Stage~1 turns PC8 so far that its best single match falls to $|r|$ 0.58 while 98.8\% of it stays in the refit basis; FA7's scores are estimates (determinacy 0.649). So name the axis by its two poles, not ``factor 8''.

\emph{Surface protein} (now for all 1,801 non-degenerate cards): each protein gets two checks, \emph{consistent across patients} (the robust rule, fixed in advance) and \emph{persists within cell types}. PC8's CD34 correlate in normal cells passes the first (median $\rho$ $+0.245$, 7/7) and fails the second ($+0.019$), so it is a cell-identity association. In malignant cells CD48, CD116, CD33 and CD146 do the same ($-0.308$, $-0.287$, $-0.272$, $+0.270$ within patients; $|\rho| \leq 0.037$ inside cell types), while CD99 (pooled $+0.222$) does the opposite: it fails the first ($+0.159$, 6/7) and passes the second ($+0.150$; 11 groups from 4 patients, a lead, not a finding). Part of every malignant-cell correlation runs through each cell's total ADT count, which CLR folds into every protein and isotype. Holding that count fixed (D29), CD63 and CD244 are the only proteins whose correlation with PC8 stays outside both isotype bands (CD63: $-0.200$ within patients, $-0.122$ inside cell types). Asked directly, without PC8, malignant cells carry less CD63 than healthy cells of the same sample and predicted type in 15 of 21 groups. Both are leads from a screen.

Descriptive: one panel for the gene poles; transductive Stage~1; seven supported patients; ADT without dsb.
\end{answerbox}

\subsection*{What we did}
We built a \emph{factor card} for every factor of 56 stored Stage 1 representations (1,810 cards, nothing refit): orientation, gene poles, per-patient held-out lift, matches across methods and K, identifiability, and cell-type and ADT associations. Table~\ref{tab:05cards} shows where the 1,810 come from. They are every factor of 56 of the atlas's 736 stored DR bases (13,540 factors in all), not every Stage~0 $\times$ Stage~1 combination: one basis per panel, plus the IFN-$\alpha$ and focus-panel bases the 2026-10-08 questions needed. How a card can be read depends on its basis's stored probe, which splits the 1,810 into two groups that do not overlap. In 10 of the 56 representations the probe is sparse (in the median supported fold at most half the factors are non-zero): their 330 cards are 119 \emph{selected drivers} (non-zero in at least one supported fold) and 211 never selected. In the other 46 the probe is dense (31 L2, 14 elastic net, 1 L1): their 1,480 cards are partial effects among many correlated factors and cannot be read factor by factor; 46 of them are never selected either. So $119 + 211 + 1{,}480 = 1{,}810$, and the never-selected cards, $211 + 46 = 257$, include the 9 degenerate ones. For IFN-$\alpha$ PCA40 and FA40 the stored probe is L1 at $C=10^{-4}$.

\textbf{Lift} is held-out AUPRC minus the patient's malignant prevalence $\pi$ (constant-score no-skill AP $=\pi$). \textbf{Univariate lift} scores cells with one factor alone, its sign fixed on the training folds. Stage 1 is \textbf{transductive} (fit on all cells, held-out patient included, without labels), so LOPO lift is label transfer, not inductive deployment.

\textbf{New in this version: factor $\times$ cell type and surface protein for every card (D27).} The cards held, for each factor, the pooled correlation with each surface protein over all cells of one label. D27 recomputes this for all 1,801 non-degenerate cards and adds the two views a pooled number cannot give: Spearman $\rho$ inside each supported patient (summarised as the median over the seven and the number that agree in sign), and inside each supported patient $\times$ predicted cell type ($\geq 30$ cells). The rule was fixed before computing: an association is \emph{robust} if the median within-patient $|\rho|$ is at least 0.20 and at least 5 of the 7 patients agree in sign. It reads the within-patient values only, so it is one check, \emph{consistent across patients}; whether an association \emph{persists within cell types} is a second check, read from the patient $\times$ cell-type median (``Two checks per protein'' below). The 9 isotype antibodies (no specific target) are the noise reference for both. Nothing is refit, so there is no $C$ policy.

\begin{explainbox}{How to read a factor}
\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{The sign is arbitrary, so orient by $\beta$.} The oriented loading is $\mathrm{sign}(\beta)\times$loading, so positive means toward malignant. PC8's $\beta$ is $-0.040$ to $-0.064$ in all five active supported folds.
\item \textbf{Numbers are labels.} Match by score correlation (Hungarian) and many-to-one $R^2$; one factor can split over two.
\item \textbf{L1 keeps one of several correlated directions.} A factor enters only if its standardized class-mean gap exceeds about $4/(C\cdot n_{\text{train}})$ (0.68--0.93\,SD across folds here); gap over threshold is the \emph{entry margin}. $\beta=0$ means below threshold, not no signal.
\item \textbf{FA loadings are generative} (how expression moves with the factor), not score weights.
\item \textbf{Check identifiability, and read ablation as attribution.}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item A relative eigengap of 10\% or less means the component can rotate.
  \item FA determinacy $\rho<0.707$ means equally valid scores may correlate only $2\rho^2-1$.
  \item Tucker $\phi$ (loading cosine) $\geq 0.95$ means the same factor; 0.85--0.94 is fair.
  \item Ablation is attribution inside the model, not causation.
  \end{itemize}
  The box ``What \emph{not identifiable} means'' below works these through for PC8 and FA7.
\item \textbf{Read a protein correlate inside patients and inside cell types} before naming a factor by it (box ``How to read $\rho$'').
\end{enumerate}
\end{explainbox}

\subsection*{What we found}
\textbf{The two lists are the two poles of one axis} (Figure~\ref{fig:05a}). The malignant pole runs from TXNIP ($+0.269$) to ELF1 ($+0.176$). The normal pole runs from IFI27 ($-0.315$) to USP18 ($-0.161$). Interferon-stimulated genes sit on both poles, so the axis contrasts two parts of the response rather than measuring ``more interferon''. FA7 agrees in sign on 19 of the 20 genes shown; IL7 is the exception. The two factors are related, not identical: PC8 has $R^2$ 0.920 on the FA40 scores, yet its score $r$ with FA7 is only $-0.685$, and their loading cosine (Tucker $\phi$) is 0.691, below the 0.85 ``fair'' band. By psychometric convention these are different factors that share a subspace: one subspace, cut two ways. PC8 recurs as factor 8 of PCA $K = 20$ ($r$ $+0.942$) and FA7 as factor 7 of FA $K = 10$ ($r$ $+0.932$); no $K = 5$ basis spans either.

\begin{explainbox}{What \emph{not identifiable} means, and why we name the axis by its poles}
\emph{In plain words.} A factor is \emph{identifiable} when the data pin its direction down uniquely: refit the same method on slightly different cells and the same factor comes back. When they do not, the data still fix a \emph{subspace}, the span of a few neighbouring factors (for PC8, itself and the components whose variances nearly tie with it), but not where one factor points inside it. Every rotation inside that subspace fits about equally well, and which one a fit returns depends on noise, the seed or a few cells. ``Factor 8'' is then a name for one arbitrary cut through the subspace. ``Neither is identifiable alone'' means this holds for PC8 and for FA7, each for its own reason; the two are related cuts through overlapping subspaces (PC8 has $R^2$ 0.920 on the FA40 scores), not the same factor.

\emph{PCA: a small eigengap.} PCA orders components by the variance they explain. PC8 explains 1.40\% of the panel's variance, PC7 1.48\% and PC9 1.26\%: PC7 is 5.5\% above it and PC9 10.0\% below (Figure~\ref{fig:05d}a; relative eigengap 0.055). When two variances are nearly tied, a small change in the data can mix the two components; the Davis--Kahan theorem bounds how far a component turns by the size of the change divided by the gap, so a small gap allows a large turn. Past PC3 this is the rule in this basis: 37 of the 40 IFN-$\alpha$ components have a neighbour within 10\%.

\emph{FA: low determinacy.} FA scores are not observed; they are estimated from the genes. Determinacy $\rho$ is the correlation between the factor and its best estimate. FA7 has 0.649, and 30 of the 36 non-degenerate FA40 factors are below 0.707 (Figure~\ref{fig:05d}b). Below 0.707, two equally valid sets of scores can correlate as little as $2\rho^2-1$; for FA7 that is $-0.16$.

\emph{What it looks like when Stage~1 is refitted} (Figure~\ref{fig:05d}c; the refits of sections \emph{Do the highest-prevalence patients shape the factors?} and \emph{Option 2}). Two numbers per refit: the best single matching factor ($|r|$) and the share of the stored axis that the whole refit basis reproduces ($R^2$). Leaving out P02, P03, P04 or all three barely moves PC8 ($|r| \geq 0.995$). Leaving out P01 drops PC8's best single match to 0.58, although $R^2$ is 0.988; patient-equal weights give 0.90 ($R^2$ 0.990). FA7 turns when P09 is left out (0.76; $R^2$ 0.981). Across all these refits $R^2 \geq 0.977$: the subspace survives, the single factor need not.

\emph{What to do.} Name the axis by both poles (the genes on each end), with its orientation and the Stage~2 penalty; compare bases by subspace ($R^2$, principal angles), not by factor number; and read ``PC8'' as shorthand for the direction, not as a unique program.
\end{explainbox}

\begin{explainbox}{How to read $\rho$: the PC8 $\times$ CD34 $+0.251$ and PC8 $\times$ CD99 $+0.222$ of report v1}
\emph{Which cells.} One label at a time, every patient together. $+0.251$ ranks the 58,549 normal cells of all 13 patients by PC8 score and by CD34 level and correlates the two rankings (Spearman). $+0.222$ does the same over the 1,569 malignant cells: one PC8 score per malignant cell is a vector of 1,569 numbers, the CD99 levels of the same cells a second one, and $\rho$ is the Pearson correlation of their ranks (Figure~\ref{fig:05rho}A--B). Neither is a malignant-vs-normal contrast.

\emph{Which score and which protein.} PC8 is oriented (multiplied by $-1$), so $\rho>0$ means cells with more of the protein sit toward the malignant-leaning pole. The protein value is the CLR of raw ADT counts: $\log(1+\text{count})$ minus the cell's mean of that quantity over all 170 antibodies, with no dsb background correction. Spearman uses ranks, so only the per-cell centring matters.

\emph{What the sign means} (Figure~\ref{fig:05rho}D). $\rho > 0$: cells ranked high on the oriented score tend to rank high on the protein, so the two rankings run the same way (forward); $\rho < 0$: they tend to rank low on it, so the rankings run in reverse; $|\rho|$ says how consistently ($\pm 1$ = exactly the same or exactly the reversed order). Among the 1,569 malignant cells, the mean CD99 rank rises from 585 in the lowest tenth of the PC8 ranking to 932 in the highest ($\rho = +0.222$), and the mean CD48 rank falls from 913 to 591 ($-0.192$); the middle rank is 785. The sign means something only because PC8 is oriented: a raw PCA sign is arbitrary, and flipping it would flip every $\rho$ and change nothing else. And because $\rho$ is computed among the cells of one label, a negative value does not make a protein ``less healthy'' or ``less malignant''. In normal cells, CD22's $-0.246$ (PC8) and $-0.312$ (FA7) within patients say that normal cells with more CD22 tend to sit toward the normal-leaning end: CD22 is highest on predicted CLP and cDC, which sit toward that end of both factors (normal-cell type means: PC8 $-0.28$ and $-0.30$, FA7 $-0.32$ and $-0.70$ cohort SD), and inside cell types the correlation shrinks to $-0.046$ (PC8) and $-0.085$ (FA7). CD34's positive sign is the same statement the other way round.

\emph{How big is $+0.25$?} Pick two normal cells at random: the one with more CD34 has the higher PC8 score about 58\% of the time instead of 50\%, and about 6\% of the rank variance is shared ($\rho^2$). That is a population tendency, not a marker that identifies the axis in a single cell. With 58,549 cells the sampling SE is about 0.004, so a p-value carries no information; inside P07's 22 malignant cells it is about 0.23.

\emph{Noise reference.} Isotype antibodies bind nothing specific, yet the same calculation gives them up to 0.109 (normal) and 0.227 (malignant) on this card, from non-specific binding and the CLR centring. A value near 0.2 must be read against that.

\emph{Pooled vs within patient.} A pooled $\rho$ mixes how cells vary inside each patient with how the patients' averages differ, in score and in staining (patients are nested in processing batches, so the two cannot be told apart). Ranking inside one patient removes the between-patient part; we report the median over the seven supported patients and how many agree in sign. Ranking inside patient $\times$ predicted cell type also removes cell identity.

\emph{Why a pooled value holds or shrinks within patients.} In normal cells every patient contributes thousands of cells over the same cell types, and the patients' score distributions overlap (section \emph{Should we aggregate patients}). Pooled and within-patient $\rho$ then agree: median ratio 0.89 over the 23,133 card $\times$ marker pairs with $|$pooled$| \geq 0.20$, and only 2 reverse sign (with a within-patient $|\rho| \geq 0.10$). In malignant cells P03 holds 758 of the 1,569 cells and patients differ in both score and staining, so a pooled $\rho$ can come from patient averages alone. There the median ratio is 0.24 over 16,190 pairs, and 707 reverse sign. D27's pooled values reproduce the cards' (D18) values to $5\times10^{-11}$ (all 10 regression checks pass), so what changes between the two numbers is the stratification, not the computation.

\emph{Confounds that remain.} Patient and batch; recipient origin (every malignant cell is recipient-derived, so recipient genotype, e.g.\ HLA-binding antibodies, is not separated from malignancy); predicted cell type (a label transferred from a healthy reference); and correlated factors across bases, so counts of robust pairs overstate independent findings.
\end{explainbox}

\textbf{What the sign means} (Figure~\ref{fig:05rho}D). Yes: the sign says whether the oriented factor's ranking of the cells and the protein's ranking run forward ($\rho > 0$) or in reverse ($\rho < 0$), and $|\rho|$ says how consistently. Panel D draws both cases for the 1,569 malignant cells: CD99 (forward, $+0.222$) and CD48 (reverse, $-0.192$). The box ``How to read $\rho$'' gives the ranks behind them, why the sign means something only because PC8 is oriented, and how to read CD22's negative and CD34's positive sign in normal cells.

\textbf{Why FA7's normal-cell and malignant-cell proteins differ} (Table~\ref{tab:05adt}). The two lists rank different cells. The normal-cell list ranks 58,549 normal cells, the largest predicted types being CLP (21\%) and MEP-EP (18\%); the malignant-cell list ranks 1,569 malignant cells, 41\% predicted HSC/MPP2, 18\% NP1 and 11\% each LMPP and HSC/MPP1. The proteins that track a factor across a population are largely those that separate the cell types present in it, so different mixes give different lists. The proteins cross over only weakly: within patients, FA7's normal-cell proteins reach CD22 $-0.099$, CD34 $+0.154$ and CD112 $+0.165$ in malignant cells, and its malignant-cell proteins reach CD116 $-0.051$, CLEC12A $+0.071$ and GPR56 $+0.013$ in normal cells; CD63 ($-0.177$) is the exception and tracks FA7 in both. Inside cell types the malignant-cell proteins also shrink (CD116 $-0.316$ to $-0.035$, CLEC12A $-0.291$ to $-0.041$, GPR56 $+0.247$ to $-0.004$; CD63 keeps more, $-0.195$ to $-0.111$). \textbf{Every card: factor $\times$ surface protein and cell type} (Figure~\ref{fig:05f}). \emph{Where the numbers come from} (MX: ``I am confused on 20274 of 289961 card x marker pairs, where do those numbers come from?''; A-20261010-33, -35). The cards are the 1,801 non-degenerate factors of the 56 Stage~1 bases of Table~\ref{tab:05cards} (PCA 430, FA 581, FactoSig 380, FactoSig-promax 410), not every Stage~0 $\times$ Stage~1 combination. Each card is paired with each of the 161 targeted proteins: $1{,}801 \times 161 = 289{,}961$ card $\times$ marker pairs per label, plus $1{,}801 \times 9 = 16{,}209$ isotype rows. Every pair is computed as in Figure~\ref{fig:05e}: one $\rho$ per supported patient, their median, the pooled value and the median inside patient $\times$ cell type. The $\rho$ and $\eta^2$ values use only the Stage~1 scores, the protein and the cell labels; Stage~2 enters in three places only. It chose the bases (47 of the 56 are, for each gene panel, the Stage~1 method and $K$ with the highest held-out median lift). It sets each card's sign (orientation by $\beta$): 584 cards have no stable sign, so panels a and b use only the 1,217 oriented ones. And it flags the 119 selected drivers. \emph{PCA is there.} No PCA card holds the strongest pair for any of the ten markers in panels a and b, but the best PCA card is close behind, at $|\rho|$ 0.55--0.69 in normal cells (against 0.69--0.81 shown) and 0.35--0.50 in malignant cells (against 0.53--0.68). PCA cards hold 2,756 of the normal-cell robust pairs and 1,377 of the malignant-cell ones. The dots are no longer coloured by method: the row label names it (MX, A-20261010-35).

\emph{Normal cells.} 20,274 of the 289,961 pairs (7.0\%, on 843 of the 1,801 cards) meet the robust rule, against 2.3\% of isotype rows under the same rule. Pooled and within-patient $\rho$ agree (Figure~\ref{fig:05f}c). The strongest are myeloid, erythroid and progenitor markers (CD11a, CD18, CD33, CD200, CLEC12A, CD123, CD151, CD71; Figure~\ref{fig:05f}a), up to $+0.809$ within patients (HVGs (500) FA40 factor 3 $\times$ CD11a, 7/7). Inside patient $\times$ cell type most of them shrink: only 729 of the 20,274 keep at least half their size (median retention 0.15). In 1,665 of 1,801 cards, predicted cell type explains more of the score within patients than patient identity explains across patients (Figure~\ref{fig:05f}d). So in normal cells a factor's surface-protein correlates mostly say which cell types the factor separates. \emph{Malignant cells.} 8,474 pairs (2.9\%, on 954 cards) meet the rule, against 1.7\% of isotype rows. Pooled values are a poor guide here: some of the strongest within-patient associations are weak when pooled (CD133 on HVGs (3000) FA40 factor 16: $+0.598$ within, 7/7, $+0.181$ pooled; Figure~\ref{fig:05f}b). Of the 8,474, 1,797 keep at least half their size inside cell types (median retention 0.26), but the malignant cell-type strata come from four patients (P02, P03, P04, P09) only. Of the 119 selected drivers, 61 have at least one robust marker in normal cells and 75 in malignant cells. Every number here is a screen over correlated factors and 161 markers, not a test.

\emph{What panel c says} (MX: ``the normal cells are not very patient specific and malignant are more patient specific, but it's coming from the rho angle, which means what?''; A-20261010-34). Panel c is about the factor--protein relation, not the factor alone. For every pair with $|$pooled $\rho| \geq 0.20$ it divides the median within-patient $\rho$ by the pooled $\rho$. In normal cells the ratio centres on 0.89: the relation seen over all patients pooled is the one seen inside each patient, because every patient brings the same cell types with the same factor--protein link. In malignant cells it centres on 0.24, and 707 of the 16,190 pairs reverse sign: most of a pooled malignant-cell $\rho$ comes from patients differing in their average score and their average staining (a Simpson's-paradox pattern), not from cells varying together inside a patient. So ``patient specific'' from the $\rho$ angle means that pooled malignant-cell protein correlates are mostly between-patient differences, with patient, sample and batch nested, which is why every protein statement in this section is read within patients. Panels d and e say the same of the scores alone: patient explains more of the score than cell type for 755 of 1,801 cards in malignant cells but 136 in normal cells.

\emph{What panels d and e plot} (MX: ``what's eta\textasciicircum 2 of patient? \ldots{} we would narrow the points to below the diagonal to be worthy of investigation''; A-20261010-37). One dot per card, every non-degenerate card (oriented or not). $\eta^2$ of patient (x) is the share of the card's score variance, among the cells of one label, that lies between patient averages: the between-patient sum of squares over the total, as in a one-way ANOVA (all 13 patients for normal cells; the 7 with at least 20 malignant cells for malignant cells). $\eta^2$ of cell type (y) is the same share for predicted cell types, computed inside each supported patient (types with at least 10 cells) and summarized by the median (in malignant cells P02, P03, P04 and P09 only). Above the diagonal cell type explains more than patient; below it patient explains more. The proposal needs one change. Below the diagonal is a patient axis: for Q1 that is a patient-specific candidate, confounded with sample and batch, not a cell-state candidate. And neither $\eta^2$ measures what is left over: for most cards most of the variance is explained by neither. PC8 sits at x = 0.010 and y = 0.105 in normal cells (0.031 and 0.120 in malignant cells), above the diagonal, yet about 90\% of its variance lies inside patient and type. The cards worth a closer look for biology beyond cell identity are near the bottom-left corner (851 normal-cell and 822 malignant-cell cards have both $\eta^2 < 0.1$), and among those the ones with held-out lift; the two checks then ask whether any protein follows that inside-type variation. For PC8 it is CD63 (``CD63, followed up'' below).

\begin{explainbox}{A statement we can make (descriptive; A-20261010-36)}
Factors learned from RNA alone, on knowledge-prior gene panels, have surface-protein correlates that reproduce across patients: in healthy cells 7.0\% of factor $\times$ protein pairs, on 843 of 1,801 factors, pass a cross-patient rule fixed in advance, against 2.3\% of isotype controls, and pooled and within-patient correlations agree (median ratio 0.89). So the factors follow hematopoietic structure that an independent modality also measures. That structure is mostly cell identity: only 729 of the 20,274 passing pairs keep at least half their size inside one patient's predicted cell type, and for 1,665 of 1,801 factors predicted cell type explains more of the score than patient does. In malignant cells, pooled correlations are dominated by differences between patients (the within-patient value is a median 0.24 of the pooled one, and 707 of 16,190 pairs reverse sign), so protein correlates of malignant-cell variation must be read within patients. \emph{It does not say} that any protein marks malignancy, that the factors are cell types (most of their variance lies inside patient and type), or anything about dsb-normalized protein; part of every correlation runs through each cell's total ADT count (``CD63, followed up'').
\end{explainbox}

\textbf{Do malignant cells carry less CD63?} (Figure~\ref{fig:05q}; A-20261010-31) MX's question: ``this is built on top of the fact that PC8 is pretty decent at classifying malignant vs.\ healthy \ldots{} So if we stretch the conclusion a bit, it's saying that malignant cells in these patients have lower CD63 expression or something, how can I say this better?'' Not from $\rho$. $\rho$ ranks malignant cells against each other, and no healthy cell enters it; PC8's AUPRC says that malignant cells, as a group, sit toward one end of PC8 compared with healthy cells. Chaining the two does not work: correlations are not transitive, and the direction in which malignant cells vary among themselves need not be the direction that separates them from healthy cells. CD99 shows it. It rises with PC8 among malignant cells ($+0.159$ within patients), yet malignant cells carry \emph{less} CD99 than healthy cells of the same sample and predicted type in 14 of 21 groups (median AUC 0.31). So D29 asks the question directly, with no factor. Inside one sample and one predicted type (the 21 groups with $\geq 10$ cells of each label, as in Figure~\ref{fig:05c}; matching the sample matters only in P09, whose malignant cells come from one of its four samples), the AUC is the chance that a malignant cell carries more of the protein than a healthy cell. Malignant cells carry less CD63 in 15 of 21 groups (median AUC 0.356; 14 of 21 with total ADT held fixed), against 5 to 8 groups for the 9 isotypes (median AUC 0.53--0.59). \textbf{Is the isotype a fair yardstick? Each cell's total ADT count} (Figure~\ref{fig:05r}; A-20261010-26, -27). CLR subtracts each cell's average log count over all 170 antibodies, so a cell with more antibody counts in total gets lower CLR values for the antibodies that bind little, isotypes most of all: isotype CLR against log total ADT, median within patient, is $-0.11$ to $-0.42$ in healthy cells and $-0.32$ to $-0.71$ in malignant cells. PC8 itself tracks total ADT counts: in healthy cells in every patient ($-0.10$ to $-0.35$), in malignant cells in P01, P02, P04 and P09 ($-0.20$ to $-0.42$) but not in P03, P06 or P07 ($|\rho| \leq 0.05$); total RNA counts much less ($|\rho| \leq 0.202$). So any antibody, isotype or not, picks up a correlation with PC8 through the cell's total count, which is how isotypes reach 0.227. Holding each cell's log total ADT count fixed (a rank partial correlation inside each patient or group; D29) shrinks PC8's malignant-cell isotype bands to 0.181 within patients and 0.070 inside cell types, both set by one isotype, RTK2071 (the other eight stay within 0.048 and 0.064), and the healthy-cell bands to 0.024 and 0.023. The proteins move too. CD146 ($+0.270 \to +0.057$), CD116 ($-0.287 \to -0.129$), CD48 ($-0.308 \to -0.177$) and CD33 ($-0.272 \to -0.177$) lose much of their within-patient value, so part of it was the total-count path. CD63 keeps most of its value ($-0.252 \to -0.200$ within patients; $-0.138 \to -0.122$ inside cell types, 9 of 11), and CD99 all of it ($+0.159 \to +0.172$; $+0.150 \to +0.130$). CD82 keeps its within-patient value ($+0.188$, 7 of 7) but halves inside cell types ($+0.143 \to +0.067$, just inside the 0.070 band). With total ADT held fixed, CD63 and CD244 ($+0.201$ within patients, 5 of 7; $+0.088$ inside cell types) are the only proteins outside both isotype bands in malignant cells. In healthy cells CD63's within-patient value disappears ($-0.111 \to -0.008$): there its link to PC8 was the total-count path, in malignant cells it is not. Holding total counts fixed is a sensitivity check, not a correction: a cell's total surface protein can be biology (cell size, activation) as much as capture, and malignant cells carry fewer total ADT counts than healthy cells of the same sample and type in 16 of 21 groups (P09: AUC 0.05--0.11 in HSC/MPP1, HSC/MPP2, LMPP and NP1).

\subsection*{Frameworks from other fields}
The factor-interpretability literature review suggests five practices worth adopting:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
\item From psychometrics: name a factor only after reporting its eigengap (Davis \& Kahan 1970), determinacy (Guttman 1955; Grice 2001) and Tucker $\phi$ against the 0.85/0.95 bands (Lorenzo-Seva \& ten Berge 2006). The cards already report all three.
\item From neuroimaging decoders: read each probe's forward pattern $\mathrm{cov}(X,s)/\mathrm{var}(s)$, which is rotation-invariant, instead of its weights (Haufe et~al.\ 2014).
\item Report selection frequency across patient resamples and seeds (Meinshausen \& B\"uhlmann 2010; Kotliar et~al.\ 2019).
\item Test ``shared'' as measurement invariance with per-patient refits (Meredith 1993); iterate with domain knowledge by target rotation to sub-signatures fixed in advance (Browne 1972).
\item Rotation itself, the team's psychology example. Varimax can make single factors identifiable when factor scores are heavy-tailed (Rohe \& Zeng 2023); the review proposes varimax-rotated PCA40 and FA as Stage~1 variants. FactoSig-promax is already an oblique rotation, but its stored loadings are the pattern matrix, and pattern and structure can rank genes differently, so the promax gene poles (allograft, apoptosis) should report both.
\end{enumerate}

{\sloppy \textbf{In the app.} The read-only factor explorer (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak mrd\_stage0\_2/\allowbreak apps/\allowbreak old34\_factor\_\allowbreak explorer.py}, port 8051) shows every card: the fold strip, lift, poles, ADT, cell type and matches. Each atlas cell links to it. It is a separate page because the atlas is a representation-by-patient heatmap, while cards are per factor and carry caveats that do not fit in a cell. The within-patient and within-cell-type ADT views of D27 are not in the app yet (proposed W7.4). Until then, any card's D27 summary is one row of \texttt{factor\_modality\_assoc\_v1/\allowbreak d27\_card\_summary.csv} (EXP-relative under \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak scorecards/\allowbreak stage2\_diagnostic/}; one row per non-degenerate card, normal and malignant columns side by side), and every robust pair is in \texttt{d27\_adt\_robust.csv} next to it.\par}

\begin{caveatbox}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
\item Transductive Stage 1; one panel for the gene poles, one seed, seven patients, no intervals (P07: 22 malignant cells).
\item Poles are not differential expression: TXNIP up or IFI27 down in malignant cells is not shown.
\item Identifiability is shown for PC8 and FA7 on IFN-$\alpha$; the refits in Figure~\ref{fig:05d}c are PCA and FA at $K = 40$ only.
\item Surface protein: CLR of raw counts, no dsb; isotype controls reach $|\rho|$ about 0.23 (99th percentile); patient is nested in batch; every malignant cell is recipient-origin. The robust rule is a screen over correlated factors and 161 markers, not a test, and the malignant cell-type strata come from four patients.
\item Part of every factor--protein correlation runs through each cell's total ADT count, which CLR folds into every antibody (D29); holding it fixed is a sensitivity check, not a correction. P09's malignant cells come from one of its four samples, so its malignant-vs-healthy contrasts use that sample only.
\item Predicted cell types are labels transferred from a healthy reference; for malignant cells they name the nearest healthy state.
\item No causal reading. Name the axis by both poles, with orientation and penalty (L1, $C=10^{-4}$).
\end{itemize}
\end{caveatbox}

\begin{teambox}
``PC8 contrasts two groups of interferon-stimulated genes. Malignant cells score toward TXNIP, IFITM2 and TENT5A and away from IFI27, PSME2 and PSMA3. FA7 is a related axis, and either one alone gives 4.8 to 19.9\pp{} of held-out lift in five of seven patients, not only P03 and P04.''

\textbf{Likely question:} ``So TXNIP is up in malignant cells?''\\
\textbf{Answer:} Not shown yet. A pole says which genes push the score, not each gene's own shift (see Next step).

\textbf{Likely question:} ``Isn't PC8 just cell type?''\\
\textbf{Answer:} In normal cells, partly: its CD34 correlate holds in every patient ($+0.245$) but vanishes inside predicted cell types ($+0.019$). Yet malignant cells score higher within 8 of 10 types, and PC8 keeps its lift inside HSC/MPP2 alone (P02 $+21.8$, P03 $+12.7$, P07 $+10.3$, P09 $+27.1$\pp).

\textbf{Likely question:} ``Is CD99 a surface marker of the malignant pole?''\\
\textbf{Answer:} Not on this evidence: within patients it is $+0.159$ (6 of 7), below the rule fixed in advance and inside the isotype noise level for malignant cells. It does persist inside cell types ($+0.150$, 9 of 11 groups from 4 patients), so it is a lead to check, not a marker.

\textbf{Likely question:} ``So malignant cells have less CD63?''\\
\textbf{Answer:} PC8's $\rho$ cannot say: it ranks malignant cells against each other. Compared directly, malignant cells carry less CD63 than healthy cells of the same sample and predicted type in 15 of 21 groups (not in P03's HSC/MPP2), as do 19 other proteins of the 161; descriptive, CLR without dsb.
\end{teambox}
