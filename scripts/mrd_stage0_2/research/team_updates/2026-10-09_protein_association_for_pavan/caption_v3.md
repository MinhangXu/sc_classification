## Full report Figure 20 (`fig5_factor_protein_to_rho_v3`): from a factor and a protein to one number, and what its sign means

(A) Stage 1 turns the RNA matrix (60,118 cells × 92 genes, each gene standardized) into a score matrix (60,118 cells × 40 factors). Column 8, multiplied by −1 and z-scored, kept for the 1,569 malignant cells, is a vector of 1,569 numbers: entry i is the PC8 score of malignant cell i. The ADT matrix (60,118 cells × 170 antibodies, raw counts) becomes CLR per cell, and its CD99 column for the same cells in the same order is a second vector of 1,569. Rank each vector (1 = lowest) and take the ordinary Pearson correlation of the two rank vectors: that is Spearman ρ, here +0.222. Any other protein (CD34, CD48, ...) goes through the same steps. (B) The arithmetic for 8 real malignant HSC/MPP2 cells of P03, one per evenly spaced PC8 quantile (the choice ignores CD99). Their ρ is +0.81, but 8 cells are far too few: all 438 cells of that group give +0.249. (C) The same number at three levels. All 1,569 malignant cells pooled (they come from the 9 patients that have any): +0.222. Inside each patient (P03's 758 cells: +0.183), summarized as the median of the 7 (+0.159; 6 of 7 positive; the exception is P07, 22 cells, −0.12). Inside each patient × predicted type group with at least 30 malignant cells (P03 HSC/MPP2, 438 cells: +0.249), summarized as the median of the 11 groups (+0.150; 9 of 11 positive). Only P02, P03, P04 and P09 have such groups. (D) What the sign means. The two rank vectors of panel A plotted against each other, for CD99 (ρ = +0.222) and CD48 (ρ = −0.192). Black line: the mean protein rank in each tenth of the PC8 ranking. The mean CD99 rank rises from 585 in the lowest tenth to 932 in the highest; the mean CD48 rank falls from 913 to 591; the middle rank is 785. So yes: the sign says whether the two rankings run forward (ρ > 0: cells ranked high on the oriented PC8 score, toward the malignant-leaning end, tend to rank high on the protein) or in reverse (ρ < 0: they tend to rank low on it), and |ρ| says how consistently (+1 or −1 = exactly the same or exactly the reversed order, 0 = no tendency). At ±0.2 the tendency shows only on average, not cell by cell. The sign means something only because PC8 was oriented; a raw PCA sign is arbitrary, and flipping it would flip every ρ and change nothing else. ρ is computed among the cells of one label, so a negative ρ does not make a protein "less malignant" or "less healthy".

## Full report Figure 22 (`fig6_two_checks_v3`): two checks per protein

One dot per surface protein (161) and isotype control (9, orange), for PC8. x = the median within-patient ρ (Table 3's middle column), y = the median within patient-and-type ρ (its last column). Gray bands: the largest |x| and |y| any isotype control reaches (malignant 0.227 and 0.120, both set by one isotype, RTK2071, drawn as the outlined diamond; healthy 0.109 and 0.043). Dotted lines: the 0.20 size bar of D27's pre-set "robust" rule (the full report's Figure 21, formerly Figure 20), which reads x only (|median| ≥ 0.20 and the same sign in at least 5 of 7 patients). That bar is lower than the malignant-cell isotype reach (0.227), so passing the rule does not by itself mean leaving the band: 5 of the 14 proteins that pass it (CD244, CD11c, CD112, CD162, CD7) stay inside, while CD48, CD116, CD33 and CD146 are outside. Dashed: y = x. This reconciles CD99 and CD48. CD48 in malignant cells passes the rule (x −0.308, 6/7, outside the band) but sits on y ≈ 0 (−0.011): inside one patient's cell type it no longer tracks PC8, so its within-patient correlation mostly says which cell types sit at which end. PC8's other strong malignant correlates do the same. CD116 (x −0.287, 7/7), CD33 (−0.272, 5/7) and CD146 (+0.270, 6/7) all pass the rule and sit outside the band, but drop to y −0.037, +0.026 and +0.017. P03, with 758 of the 1,569 malignant cells, sits near 0 on all three (−0.031, +0.065, −0.016), so their pooled values are only −0.108, −0.137 and +0.090. CD99 fails the rule on size (x +0.159, inside the 0.227 band) but keeps most of that inside cell types (y +0.150, 9 of 11 groups positive, above the 0.120 band). Both statements hold, and neither is a significance test. Three cautions keep CD99 a lead rather than a finding. Its y rests on 11 groups from 4 patients (P03 alone supplies 3 groups and 745 of the 1,267 cells). One isotype sets both bands. And 12 of the 161 proteins sit outside the y band, with larger |y| for CD117 (−0.172) and CD71 (−0.152). Of the 14 proteins that pass the rule in malignant cells, only CD63 and CD82 are outside both bands. (The CD99 literature is task W9.3, to be checked before any text cites it.) In healthy cells every protein falls to |y| ≤ 0.10: PC8's healthy-cell protein correlates are mostly a cell-type effect (v2 Figure 2).

## What the sign means

Both factors are oriented so that positive points to the malignant-leaning end. ρ is computed among the cells of one label only, so its sign tells which way the protein runs along the factor inside that group: ρ > 0, the factor's ranking of the cells and the protein's ranking run forward (the same way); ρ < 0, they run in reverse; |ρ| says how consistently (Figure 20 panel D). In healthy cells, CD34 is positive (PC8 +0.245, FA7 +0.298 within patients): healthy cells with more CD34 tend to sit toward the malignant-leaning end. CD22 is negative (PC8 −0.246, FA7 −0.312): healthy cells with more CD22 tend to sit toward the healthy-leaning end. In other words, the factor's ranking of healthy cells partly reverses their CD22 ranking. It is a weak tendency, not a mirror image. It does not mean CD22 is "less associated with healthy cells": no malignant cell enters that ρ, and comparing labels is a different question (Figure 19 asks it of the score itself). Flipping the orientation would flip every sign and change nothing else. Here the sign comes mostly from cell types. CD22 is highest on predicted CLP and cDC, which sit toward the healthy-leaning end of both factors (healthy-cell type means: PC8 −0.28 and −0.30 SD, FA7 −0.32 and −0.70). Inside one patient's cell type the correlation shrinks to −0.046 (PC8) and −0.085 (FA7).

## Why FA7's healthy-cell and malignant-cell proteins differ

The two lists rank different cells. The healthy list ranks 58,549 healthy cells, the largest predicted types being CLP (21%) and MEP-EP (18%). The malignant list ranks 1,569 malignant cells: 41% predicted HSC/MPP2, 18% NP1, and 11% each LMPP and HSC/MPP1. The proteins that track a factor across a population are largely the ones that separate the cell types present in it, so different mixes give different lists. The proteins cross over only weakly. Within patients, FA7's healthy-cell proteins in malignant cells are CD22 −0.099, CD34 +0.154 and CD112 +0.165. Its malignant-cell proteins in healthy cells are CD116 −0.051, CLEC12A +0.071 and GPR56 +0.013. CD63 is the exception at −0.177, so it tracks FA7 in both labels. The malignant-cell proteins also shrink inside cell types: CD116 −0.316 to −0.035, CLEC12A −0.291 to −0.041, GPR56 +0.247 to −0.004. CD63 keeps more (−0.195 to −0.111). So in both labels FA7's protein correlates mostly say which cell types sit at each end of the factor, and the two lists need not reflect two different biologies.
