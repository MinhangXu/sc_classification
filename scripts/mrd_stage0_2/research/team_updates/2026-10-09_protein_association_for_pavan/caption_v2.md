# Captions for the v2 figures (PC8 x surface protein)

PC8 = factor 8 of a PCA (K = 40) on the Hallmark interferon-alpha genes, fit on RNA only. Its sign was set by the malignant-vs-healthy classifier, so positive = the malignant-leaning pole. Protein = CITE-seq counts, CLR per cell. Predicted cell type = reference-mapped annotation. Descriptive; the 7 patients with at least 10 malignant cells.

**Figure 1. Which surface proteins track PC8, patient by patient** (`fig1_proteins_by_patient_v2`). Each square is the Spearman correlation between PC8 score and protein level among one patient's healthy (top) or malignant (bottom) cells. Rows are the ten largest |median| correlations plus the proteins named in our report (†); the cut at ten is arbitrary (next in healthy cells: CD43, CD45RO, CD244, about +0.14). In healthy cells the same proteins recur in 6 or 7 of 7 patients (|median| 0.15 to 0.25), mostly because cell types differ (Figure 2). Read each value against its patient's isotype row, not zero: with 11,815 cells (P01 healthy) an isotype at 0.19 is background, not chance. Malignant values vary more, and CD99 (+0.16) sits inside the malignant isotype floor (0.23), so it is not established.

**Figure 2. Why CD34 tracks PC8: cell types differ, not cells within a type** (`fig2_cd34_between_within_v2`). Every cell passed a CD34+ sort gate, yet raw CD34 still varies widely (5th to 95th percentile 5 to 340 over all 60,118 cells), from HSC/MPP down to committed types such as ncMono (per-type medians in `fig2a_celltype_means_v2.csv`). (A) Types with more CD34 also score higher on PC8 (Spearman +0.60 across 13 types), which can account for the median within-patient correlation of +0.24 (range +0.14 to +0.38) when types are mixed. (B) Inside one patient's cell type (71 groups of at least 30 healthy cells) the correlation is near zero (median +0.02), except MEP-EP (+0.30 to +0.61, all 7 patients) and, weakly, CLP (+0.08 to +0.19). (C) The other healthy-cell proteins of Figure 1 shrink the same way, to about ±0.06 or less.

**Figure 3. Malignant cells score higher than healthy cells of the same type in the same patient** (`fig3_malignant_vs_healthy_same_type_v2`). Each row in B is one patient and one predicted cell type with at least 10 malignant and 10 healthy cells: the dot is the malignant minus the healthy mean PC8 score (panel A works one example through), the line a 95% bootstrap interval over cells. Matching patient and type removes differences between patients and between cell types; outside P09 the groups also share sample and batch, and in P09 keeping only healthy cells from the malignant cells' samples leaves all signs unchanged. Malignant cells score higher in 19 of 21 (median +0.41 cohort SD), though only 10 of 21 intervals exclude zero and malignancy is not separated from recipient (versus donor) origin. For the user: the earlier "19 of 20" left out pDC2 · P06 (−0.004) only because that figure showed types with at least 20 malignant cells cohort-wide.

**Figure 4. How the noise floor is set** (`fig4_isotype_noise_floor_v2`). The data names 9 of the 170 antibodies as isotype controls by clone ('Isotype-MOPC.21', 'Isotype-RTK2071', ...; the other 161 are 'ADT-<target>'). They share the class and barcode chemistry of the real antibodies but have no target on human cells, and are added to measure non-specific binding. Each dot is one antibody's median within-patient ρ with PC8; the floor is the largest isotype |median|: 0.11 in healthy cells (RTK4530; 33 of 161 proteins outside) and 0.23 in malignant cells, set by RTK2071 alone (0.16 without it; 9 of 161 outside). All 9 isotypes sit above zero, so part of the background is a shared shift. The floor is a yardstick we chose, not a significance test.
